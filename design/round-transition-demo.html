Agents deployed

The synthetic search begins.

Project ATLAS sends the agents into a closed demo environment. They are not touching real customers, real controls, or production endpoints. They are testing generated cases against a mock risk scorer and recording each proposed defensive fix.

In practice, this is the evaluation moment a PM cares about: can the team find a model vulnerability before it reaches users, and can the work stay bounded by scoring-query and action-rate limits?

Round 1 response

The first answer is not always the right answer.

Red-team surfaces an under-ranked synthetic cohort. Bank-defense proposes a decision-threshold fix, but the judge rejects it because a fix must work beyond the exact cases that exposed the issue.

The useful signal is the rejection. The system found a weakness, then prevented the team from treating a narrow improvement as a production-ready answer.

Round 2 response

Defense iterates with a stronger candidate.

The defense proposes a synthetic feature or calibration change. The judge reruns generalization checks, including locked holdouts, and verifies that added reviews or declines stay within the action-rate limit.

This is the loop mature AI teams need: learn from the failed fix, improve model behavior, and prove the improvement survives data the agents did not see.

Round 3 · final report

The evidence becomes a decision record.

The judge consolidates miss-rate movement, generalization, and customer-friction trade-offs into a deterministic verdict. The final report is not an agent summary; it is the record of what changed and why it passed.

This is the artifact you would want before moving from research preview into a governance, model-risk, or executive review conversation.

Evaluation criteria

Every defensive fix has to pass three tests.

First, it must reduce model miss rate. Second, it must generalize beyond the cohort that exposed the issue. Third, it must respect the action-rate limit so customer experience does not quietly degrade.

If one of those checks fails, the fix is not ready. The agents can recommend; deterministic code decides.
